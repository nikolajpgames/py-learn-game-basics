Session 7 Arc 1 · First Pixels

Drawing By Numbers

You have a window. Now you get to put things in it — and find out where the computer thinks “down” is.

What you're making

Rectangles, circles and lines, anywhere you want them. By the end you'll draw something of your own out of nothing but shapes.

Every game on earth is this, done very fast, with better shapes.

New this session

Every pixel has an address: two numbers, x and y. x is how far across, y is how far down.

Here's the part that catches everyone out: y counts downwards. The top-left corner is (0, 0). Going down the screen makes y get bigger. It's upside-down compared to graphs in maths, and it's like that on nearly every computer ever built.

So (0, 0) is the top-left, and (800, 450) is the bottom-right corner of your window.

Turn the knobs

All of today's knobs are the numbers inside the drawing lines.

rect(x, y, w, h)Top-left corner, then width and height. Negative width does nothing useful — try it and see.
circle(…, r)The point is the middle, and r is the radius. A radius of 0 draws nothing at all.
line(…, w)The last number is thickness. Try 1, try 40.
FPS = 60How many times a second the loop goes round. Nothing is moving yet, so changing it looks like nothing — its only job right now is to stop the program running flat out and warming up your computer.
BG = (20, 22, 34)Still there from Session 6. Try making the background the same colour as a shape and watch the shape vanish.

Stuck?

Hint 1 — my new shape isn't showing up
Three usual suspects. Is it drawn after screen.fill? (fill paints over everything before it.) Is it the same colour as the background? Is it off the edge of the window — coordinates bigger than 800 or 450?
Hint 2 — which order do the numbers go in?

rect wants a corner and a size: (x, y, width, height).
circle wants a middle and a radius: (x, y), radius.
line wants two points: (x1, y1), (x2, y2).

Every one of them takes screen first and the colour second. That never changes.

Hint 3 — where exactly is the middle?
Half of 800 is 400. Half of 450 is 225. So the centre of your window is (400, 225). Better still, write (WIDTH // 2, HEIGHT // 2) — then it stays in the middle even if you resize the window later.

Challenge

Draw the Geometry Dash cube. A square, two smaller squares for eyes, and a darker square inside for the face. Four or five rectangles, that's all it takes.

Get it looking right and don't delete it — that cube is going to be the thing you're jumping over spikes with around Session 32.

How it could break

TypeError: function missing required argument A drawing function didn't get everything it needs. Count the things inside the brackets and compare with the steps above — something got left out, usually the thickness on a line.
TypeError: invalid color argument The colour needs its own brackets and exactly three numbers: (255, 0, 0). Two numbers or four won't do.
The shape is there but the window is one solid colour You drew it, then screen.fill painted right over the top. Drawing has to come after the fill.
IndentationError: unexpected indent Your new line doesn't start in the same column as the ones around it. Line the left edges up exactly.
The circle is an oval / in the wrong place You gave circle a corner instead of a centre. It wants the middle of the circle.

Stop while it still runs.
