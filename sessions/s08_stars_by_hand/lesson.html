Three Stars By Hand

Three stars, three variables, three nearly identical lines. Then you get rid of two of them.

What you're making

A night sky with three stars in it, written out one star at a time. Then the same night sky in a lot less code.

You end up with exactly the same picture and four fewer lines of code.

New this session

A tuple glues a few values into one thing. star1 = (100, 80) keeps an x and a y together so they travel as one star instead of two loose numbers. Round brackets.

A list holds many things under one name. Square brackets. stars = [star1, star2, star3] and now three separate names are one name.

And a for loop can walk through a list, handing you one item at a time. That is what turns three near-identical draw lines into one.

Stuck?

Hint 1 — what does the loop line actually do?

for star in stars: means "take each thing out of stars, one at a time, call it star, and run the indented lines".

So with four stars in the list, the draw line runs four times — with star holding a different tuple each go. You write it once; Python repeats it.

Hint 2 — how do I get the x back out of a star?
By its position. star[0] is the first number you put in, star[1] is the second. Counting starts at 0, the same as it did with range in Session 4.
Hint 3 — why are there two sets of brackets?

In stars = [(100, 80), (400, 200)] the square brackets are the list, and each pair of round brackets is a tuple inside it.

So the list has two entries, and each entry has two numbers. Two levels, two bracket shapes. The picture above shows the same thing.

Challenge

Make a constellation. Look up a real one — Orion, the Plough, Cassiopeia — and place the stars roughly where they belong.

Then join them up with pygame.draw.line between the pairs that connect, using the same star[0], star[1] trick to get at each one's position.

Ten or twelve stars is plenty. And notice that at ten, adding one more is still just one line — which it very much would not have been an hour ago.

How it could break

NameError: name 'star4' is not defined You put star4 in the list but never made it, or spelled it differently in the two places. Python will not guess.
IndexError: tuple index out of range You asked for star[2] but the tuple only has two numbers in it, at positions 0 and 1.
TypeError: 'int' object is not subscriptable Your list holds loose numbers rather than tuples — [100, 80] instead of [(100, 80)]. Then star is a single number and star[0] means nothing.
IndentationError: expected an indented block The draw line after for star in stars: has to be pushed in, exactly like the lines inside an if.
Only one star appears The draw line is not indented, so it sits outside the loop and runs once at the end — with whatever star happened to be last.
Nothing appears at all The list is empty, or the loop is drawing before screen.fill rather than after it.

Stop while it still runs.
